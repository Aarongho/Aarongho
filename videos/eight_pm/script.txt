At 8:01, my little sister's window was still open. And something was climbing in.
You see, in this town, at exactly 8 PM, every window and every door must be shut. Or else. That's why school ends at 7. Pizza places stop delivering at 7:30. And last year, Mr. Barnes forgot to close his cat flap. In the morning, his house was empty. Even the cat was gone.
But last night, Mom and Dad were working late. My name is Eddie, I'm thirteen, and I was in charge of locking the house.
7:58. I ran through the house. Front door. Click. Back door. Click. Kitchen window. Slam. 7:59. Bathroom window. Slam. 8:00. Dong. Dong. The town bell. Outside, every streetlight turned red.
Then I heard it. Knock. Knock. Knock. Something was walking down our street, knocking on every door. Knock. Knock. It stopped at our house. The doorknob turned. Locked. It moved to the window. Tap. Tap. Locked.
Then, upstairs, I heard a creak. And I felt a cold breeze. My little sister's window was wide open.
I ran upstairs. Lily was asleep. Ten long, pale fingers curled over the windowsill. Then a face rose into the window. Huge black eyes. A smile way too wide.
Then I noticed something. Its fingers stopped at the edge of the windowsill. It couldn't cross the window frame by itself. It had to be invited. And an open window is an invitation.
So I grabbed the window and slammed it shut. Slam! It screamed and vanished into the dark.
I hugged Lily. We were safe. Then she woke up and whispered. Eddie, I didn't open my window.
Then who did?
Behind us, the closet door creaked open.
