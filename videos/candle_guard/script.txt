A birthday kid blew out three of our town's magic candles, and the whole town turned grey! Then every dog vanished! Poof!
You see, in this town, twelve candles burn in the middle of the square, and they must never go out. Every candle that goes out takes something away. That's why birthday cakes are illegal. Firefighters aren't allowed downtown. And in 1998, my neighbor sneezed near the candles. We haven't had Tuesdays since.
But yesterday was my first day as a candle guard. My name is Eddie, and I'm thirteen. And the mayor's son, Kevin, was turning twelve.
Kevin saw the candles. He smiled. He took a deep breath. Kevin, no! Phoo! Three candles went out. Poof! All the colours vanished. Poof! Every dog in town disappeared. Poof! Grandma's teeth were gone. My teef!
Then the wind started blowing. Whoosh! Candle four. The school vanished. The kids cheered. Whoosh! Candle five. The roads were gone. Whoosh! Candle six. The sun disappeared! It was night in the middle of lunch!
I covered the candles with my body. Nobody breathe! Then it started raining. Tss! Seven. Everybody's voices vanished. Tss! Eight. The mayor's pants. Tss! Nine, ten, eleven! The houses, the trees, even the ground started fading!
One candle left. And it was shrinking.
Then I noticed something. Kevin was still holding his birthday cake. Twelve candles. The rule never said which twelve candles. Just twelve candles, burning in the middle of town.
So I grabbed the cake, lit every candle with the last flame, and held it over my head. Fwoosh! Twelve new flames. Poof! The sun came back. Poof! The houses. Poof! The dogs, the roads, the mayor's pants. Grandma got her teeth back. Even Tuesday came back. Everybody cheered.
The mayor shook my hand. Eddie, you saved our town! Then Kevin looked at the cake. He took a deep breath.
